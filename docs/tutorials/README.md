# How to use Foray Planner

[Foray Planner](https://forayplanner.com/) turns iNaturalist fungi observations into trip
planning: where your target mushrooms are being found right now, which trails and free camps
are closest to them, and how to string several spots into a road trip.

Four short walkthroughs, each with an animated overview and step-by-step screenshots:

1. [Getting started](#1-getting-started) - set your location, pick months and genera, read the results
2. [Destination details](#2-destination-details) - map layers, calendar, photos, trails, campgrounds, public land
3. [Plan a road trip](#3-plan-a-road-trip) - shortlist spots, pin a campground, export the route
4. [On your phone](#4-on-your-phone) - the same flows on mobile

> Foray Planner is a planning and mapping tool. It makes no identification, edibility, or
> safety claims, and it doesn't assert that camping is allowed anywhere: it shows land
> ownership and links the official source. Species information lives on each taxon's
> iNaturalist page.

## 1. Getting started

![Getting started walkthrough](getting-started.gif)

**1. Open the site.** The map shows destinations near your home location, and the panel on
the left ranks them, best first.

![The map and ranked destinations](img/getting-started-01-overview.png)

**2. Set your location.** Type a town, park or address into **Search location…** and pick a
suggestion. You can also click the 📍 button to use your device's location, or click anywhere
on the map. The list re-ranks around your new home straight away.

![Results re-ranked around a new home](img/getting-started-02-home-set.png)

**3. Choose your months.** The **Months** pill (it shows the current month) sets when you'll be
out. Select several months to plan a longer trip.

![The Months picker](img/getting-started-03-months.png)

**4. Pick target genera.** **All genera** ranks every fungus. To focus, open the pill, search
for a genus (for example *Cantharellus*, chanterelles) and pick it. Add as many as you like;
remove one with its ✕. Your picks are remembered on this device.

![Filtering to chanterelles](img/getting-started-04-genera.png)

**5. Read a destination card.** Each card leads with a plain-language line explaining why it
ranks: which genus is active, whether it's at peak, building, or past peak, how many records
fall in your months, recent rain, and distance to the nearest trailhead. Below that are the
score bar, species count, recent sightings, elevation and rainfall, then the top genera.

![A destination card](img/getting-started-05-card.png)

**6. Select a destination.** Click a card to fly the map there. Numbered pink markers are
precise (verified-location) observations; click one for the iNaturalist record.

![A selected destination on the map](img/getting-started-06-selected.png)

**7. Adjust radius and sort.** **Radius** sets how far from home to search. **Sort** switches
between *Best overall*, *Active now* (what's been seen in the last few weeks), and *Nearest*.

![The Radius presets](img/getting-started-07-radius.png)

![Sort options](img/getting-started-08-sort.png)

## 2. Destination details

![Destination details walkthrough](region-details.gif)

**1. Turn on map layers.** The **Layers** pill overlays land ownership (BLM, USFS, tribal,
state and other federal), campgrounds and dispersed sites, fire perimeters and burn scars,
contour lines, and aerial imagery.

![The Layers menu](img/region-details-01-layers.png)

![Forest Service and BLM land shaded on the map](img/region-details-02-land-layer.png)

**2. Open Details.** Every card has a **Details** button. It opens five tabs for that
destination.

**3. Calendar.** Which genera have been recorded here in each month, and how many records.
Good for spotting when a place's season starts and ends.

![The Calendar tab](img/region-details-03-calendar.png)

**4. Photos.** Recent research-grade iNaturalist photos from the destination, with the
observer's credit and a link to each record.

![The Photos tab](img/region-details-04-photos.png)

**5. Trails.** Hiking paths, forest roads and trailheads, sorted by relevance: named routes
and trails with the most nearby finds come first. "192 nearby" counts target-genus records
within 500 m of the trail.

![The Trails tab](img/region-details-05-trails.png)

**6. Campgrounds.** Developed campgrounds (Recreation.gov) and reported campsites
(OpenStreetMap), closest first. "free" is shown only when the source explicitly says no fee.

![The Campgrounds tab](img/region-details-06-camps.png)

**7. Public land.** Who manages the land nearby. Check the managing agency for access and
camping rules.

![The Public land tab](img/region-details-07-land.png)

**8. Draw a trail.** Click a trail in the list to draw the whole trail on the map. **← Back to
results** returns to the ranked list.

![A selected trail drawn on the map](img/region-details-08-trail-selected.png)

## 3. Plan a road trip

![Road trip walkthrough](plan-a-trip.gif)

**1. Shortlist destinations.** Click **+ Plan** on each card you want to visit (it changes to
**✓ In route**). Your picks are counted in the bar at the bottom of the panel.

![Three destinations shortlisted](img/plan-a-trip-01-shortlist.png)

**2. (Optional) Pin an exact stop.** By default a stop is the middle of the destination. To
drive to a specific campground, trail or public-land parcel instead, open that destination's
**Details**, select the row, and click **📍 Use … as trip stop**.

![A campground pinned as the trip stop](img/plan-a-trip-02-pin.png)

**3. Plan the route.** Click **Plan a route**. Your stops are ordered into a trip from your
home, drawn on the map, and each gets a stop card. You can set a start and destination, the
maximum stops, the longest leg you want to drive, and "Free camp" only.

![The planned route](img/plan-a-trip-03-route.png)

**4. Check each stop.** A stop card shows the leg and total distance, the score, what's active,
the nearest camp and trail, and a fire warning if one is near. **Choose stop point** jumps to
that stop's Details to pin a different point.

![A stop card](img/plan-a-trip-04-stop.png)

**5. Export.** **⬇ GPX** downloads the route for any maps or GPS app, **⬇ JSON** downloads the
raw plan, and **Open in Google Maps** opens turn-by-turn directions through every stop.

![Export buttons](img/plan-a-trip-05-export.png)

> Distances are straight-line estimates, not road routing. Use your maps app for actual
> driving directions.

## 4. On your phone

![Mobile walkthrough](mobile.gif)

**1. The map fills the screen.** Results live in a sheet at the bottom; the search bar and
filter pills float on top.

![The mobile map](img/mobile-01-map.png)

**2. Drag the sheet up** by its handle to see the ranked destinations. Drag it down again to
get back to the map.

![The results sheet opened](img/mobile-02-sheet-open.png)

**3. Details work the same as on desktop**: Calendar, Photos, Trails, Campgrounds and Public
land.

![Details on mobile](img/mobile-03-details.png)

![The Trails tab on mobile](img/mobile-04-trails.png)

**4. Build a trip** with **+ Plan** and **Plan a route** at the foot of the sheet.

![Destinations shortlisted on mobile](img/mobile-05-plan.png)

Tip: add Foray Planner to your home screen (your browser's "Install app" or "Add to Home
Screen") to open it full-screen like an app.

## Updating these tutorials

Everything on this page is generated by [`record.py`](record.py), which drives a real browser
through each walkthrough against the live site. When the UI changes, re-record with:

```bash
just tutorials                 # all four
just tutorials plan-a-trip     # just one
```

It needs `ffmpeg`. Captions are part of the script, so edit them there.
